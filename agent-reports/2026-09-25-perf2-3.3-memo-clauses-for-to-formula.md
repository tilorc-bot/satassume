## Change

- `TemplateRegistry.clauses_for` memoizes `expr -> (compiled, formulas)` in
  the registry. The memo holds up to 50,000 entries, is cleared when full, and
  is also cleared by `register` together with the MRO cache.
- `sympy_api._formula(expr, relations)` memoizes `to_formula` on SymPy
  Booleans, including the `Unsupported` failures (their category and message
  are stored, and a fresh exception is raised on each hit). It holds up to
  100,000 entries and is cleared whenever the default registry's `version`
  (item 3.2) changes. `_ask` calls it for the proposition and the
  assumptions.

Correctness:

- **Templates.** They are pure functions of the expression: they read its
  structure and SymPy's own `is_*` on numeric atoms, never the engine. The
  engine never mutates the returned lists (`node()` copies before appending
  extension facts). So a memoized result equals a recomputed one until a
  template is registered, and registering clears the memo.
- **`to_formula`.** It depends on the expression, the `relations` flag (part
  of the key) and, through the scope of custom and scalar-like predicates, on
  the default registry. Every registry change bumps its version. SymPy
  equality is the same key semantics the answer memo already uses.

## Risks for review

- A template that consults engine state or mutable global state would now be
  stale. None does today. The ones that call `.is_*` do so on numeric atoms
  through SymPy's own cached assumptions.
- Both memos hold references to SymPy expressions up to their bounds (50k and
  100k entries).
- The memo is module-level in `sympy_api` and keyed on the *default*
  registry, which is also what `to_formula` itself consults. An engine with a
  private `Extensions` does not change `to_formula`'s scope today either.
