# Agent report: item 3.1, the `implied` misses (measured, both halves dropped)

- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Status:** dropped by the plan's first rule; neither half reaches 5%
  of the replay, and the engine-side batching the plan sketched does not
  exist as described (the root units do not come from the write-back).
  Nothing implemented. Script `agent-reports/scripts/assume_misses.py`,
  committed on `perf-solver` with this report.
- **Scope:** `satassume/solver.py` (`_assume`, `_attach_held`, `_solve`
  with theories), `satassume/engine.py` (`Session.query_literal`,
  `writeback`, node addition), all unchanged
- **Read this if:** you want the exact origin and cost of every `implied`
  recompute on the refine stream, what holding assumption levels with a
  theory attached would recover, and why the root-unit half is smaller
  than the miss count suggests

## Measurement

`assume_misses.py`, one instrumented pass (`tools/query_log.py`'s
wrappers underneath; answers identical): `Solver._assume` (the engine of
`implied` and of the propagation stage of `entails`) is wrapped; a call is
a hit if answered from the held levels or the propagation cache, a miss if
`_assume_propagate` ran, and each miss is classified from the solver's
state before the call. For theory sessions the script also times, before
each `entails` solve, `_assume_propagate(A)` followed by `_backtrack(0)`:
the work of deciding and propagating the assumptions from root (theory
asserts included) that the search is about to redo and that a held level
would save.

Local machine, cold replay 4.22 s; 14,189 `_assume` calls, 334 ms in all
(7.9% of the replay); 8,835 hits (11 ms), 5,354 misses (323 ms):

| miss class (state before the call) | n | ms | ms/call | share of replay |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| theory session (levels never held) | 1,402 | 133 | 0.095 | 3.2% |
| root trail grew since the last call (a unit clause) | 1,895 | 110 | 0.058 | 2.6% |
| a search ran since and did not keep the levels | 814 | 37 | 0.046 | 0.9% |
| levels dropped by a clause insertion (`_attach_held` fallback) | 907 | 35 | 0.039 | 0.8% |
| changed assumption set, first call, other | 336 | 9 | | 0.2% |

The plan's split (about 1,750 root units, 1,646 theory sessions, the rest
first calls and changed sets) is confirmed in shape: 1,895 and 1,402.

**Theory half.** The 1,402 theory-session misses by cause: an atom was
registered since 587 (71 ms; `register_atom` returns to root and holding
cannot keep that), same assumptions and nothing changed 366 (28.7 ms), a
search since 243 (17.5 ms; a held-capable `_solve` would keep the levels
as it does without theories), root grew 116 (9 ms), changed set or first
90 (7 ms). Search side: the 1,564 theory-session `entails` solves (551
ms) each re-propagate A from root at 0.044 ms, 68 ms in all, 0 conflicts.
**Recoverable by holding levels with a theory attached: 28.7 + 17.5 + 68
= 114 ms, 2.7% of the replay.** What would break, for the record: the
theory keeps its levels pushed between public calls (allowed by the
contract as written: the theory level always equals the solver level, and
LRA and EUF undo by trail, so a later `pop_level` is exact), but
`_attach_held` propagates without the theory, so a clause attached while
held could imply a theory atom that is never asserted; it would need
`_tpropagate` there with a theory conflict dropping the levels, and
`check_protocol` in the theory tests asserts level 0 at the end of every
trace. None of that is worth 2.7%.

**Root-unit half.** Where the units come from: not `Session.writeback()`,
which copies the solver's new root facts *out* to the engine cache
(`engine.py` 428-440). A live session's root grows when a node is added
(`ensure`, escalation) and its cached facts are emitted as unit clauses
(`engine.py` 197-199: `engine.cache.facts(node)` emitted through
`_add_clauses` as unit clauses; 319 for a custom atom's cached value), and
when a search learns a unit. Those units are needed by the very query that adds
the node, so "batching at session creation or after the query" has
nothing to batch; the growth is inherent to adding nodes with known
facts. Solver side: the plan's alternative (insert the unit at root and
keep the held levels) is valid only if the unit implies nothing new at the
held levels; the script compares the recomputed trail with the old held
trail plus the new root literals: **valid for 516 of the 1,895 (23.6 ms,
0.6% of the replay)**, invalid for 1,169 (77.5 ms), and the assumption
set changed as well in 210. Even the whole class is 2.6%.

## Decision

Dropped, both halves: theory hold 2.7%, solver-side root-unit insertion
0.6%, the entire root-growth class 2.6%, the entire `implied` recompute
cost 7.7% but spread over causes no single change addresses. No code
change, no A/B, gate2 or test run; the theory-mode variant of the fuzz is
not needed without a solver change.

## Risks for review

- The theory-half bound counts the propagation of A once per solve at the
  measured 0.044 ms; the search then decides the target literal on top,
  which a held level does not save. The bound is an upper bound.
- The `_attach_held` fallback class (907 misses, 35 ms) is the one
  solver-side item not in the plan: a clause inserted while levels are
  held that is satisfied only by a literal of a higher level, or unit at
  a lower held level, drops the levels. 0.8%; recorded, not pursued.
- `assume_misses.py` reads `ctx.entails` from `tools/query_log.py` to
  tell `entails`' own `_assume` call from `implied`'s; same maintenance
  note as the other scripts.
