- **Date:** 2026-09-25
- **Status:** measurement only, on the rebased `perf-solver` HEAD (with
  the engine batch: 3.2, 3.3, 2.5). Script
  `agent-reports/2026-09-perf-rounds/scripts/rule_propagation_share.py`. Completes the engine
  agent's 2.3 materialization measurement with the solver side.
- **Scope:** `satassume/solver.py` (`_propagate`, the insertion methods),

- **Read this if:** you decide whether the rules-as-propagator item (plan
  2.3) is worth a separate agent

## Measurement

Read-only wrappers on the replay (answers identical). Every problem
clause is tagged by the insertion call the engine used:
`add_pattern(RULE_INTERNAL, ...)` is the rule block (one per node),
`add_internal` the compiled structural templates, `add_clauses` /
`add_clause` everything else (cached facts, the assumption formula, cone
clauses); learnt and theory clauses by their class. `_propagate` is
wrapped: the trail slice it processes gives the watch lists it scanned,
counted by clause tag, and the literals it assigns give their reason
clauses by tag. Propagation time on rule clauses is the `_propagate` time
times the rule share of watch-list visits (the cost of visiting a clause
in a watch list does not depend on its tag). Local machine, cold pass on
this HEAD 3.59 s.

| | rule block | templates | other | learnt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| clauses inserted | 647,761 | 119,376 | 13,827 | |
| insertion time | 358 ms (10.0%) | 235 ms (6.5%) | 108 ms (3.0%) | |
| watch-list visits | 1,630,538 (82.9%) | 253,392 (12.9%) | 75,301 (3.8%) | 8,289 (0.4%) |
| assignments by reason | 621,452 (81.7%) | 94,069 (12.4%) | 41,618 (5.5%) | 3,800 (0.5%) |

`_propagate`: 114,694 calls, 818 ms inner time (22.8% of the cold pass);
attributable to rule clauses **678 ms, 18.9%**. Rule-block insertion
**358 ms, 10.0%** (the solver side of what the engine agent measured as
11.2% materialization pre-2.5; the compile of the pattern is shared and
already hoisted).

**Combined: about 1.04 s, 28.9% of the cold pass** is spent inserting and
propagating rule clauses. That is the share the propagator *replaces*,
not the share it removes: 81.7% of all propagated assignments are rule
implications, and a propagator must still derive them. What it removes
is the insertion (10.0%) and the difference between scanning two to
three watched clauses per visit and a table lookup per assigned
predicate literal (each rule implication is a fixed pair `pred -> pred'`
of the same node; a propagator holds one implication list per predicate
index and reasons are the fixed binary clause). A conservative bound is
therefore the insertion plus half of the propagation share, about 19%;
the ceiling is 28.9%.

## Decision

Measurement only; nothing implemented. The plan's decision rule for 2.3
("implement only if the unary-rule share is 10% or more") is cleared by a
wide margin: the rule block is the largest single cost of the replay on
this HEAD. Recommendation to the orchestrator: propose the propagator to
the user; it is the one item left with a real gain and it is a design
change (a theory hook whose reasons are the fixed binary rule clauses, so
conflict analysis is unchanged; the fuzz gets a mode running the same
problems with rules as clauses and as a propagator, comparing `implied`
and `entails`).

## Risks for review

- The watch-visit count is taken after each `_propagate` call from the
  watch lists as they then are; clauses moved between watch lists during
  the call are counted where they ended up. The tag shares are robust to
  that (82.9% visits, 81.7% reasons agree).
- The inner `_propagate` time includes the `perf_counter` pair per call
  (114,694 calls, about 20 ms).
- The insertion time of the rule block is solver-side only; the engine's
  bookkeeping around it (`nclauses`, `ensure_vars`) is not counted.
